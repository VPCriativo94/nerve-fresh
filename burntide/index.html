The dose gap is the thing to understand

The vinegar trial the page leans on gave people 750 to 1,500 mg of acetic acid a day for twelve weeks — that is 15 to 30 ml of liquid vinegar, drunk daily. One Burntide gummy contains 525 mg of blend in total, and that 525 mg is shared between the apple cider vinegar and three separate BHB salts. The label never says how much acetic acid is in a gummy, so nobody reading it — including us — can line this product up against that trial.

The label's own numbers point the same way for the BHB side: it lists 1 mg of calcium and 0.75 mg of magnesium per gummy, both at 0% of the daily value. Those are the minerals the BHB salts are built on.

None of this makes the product something other than what it is: a low-calorie vinegar gummy sold with the manufacturer's stated 60-day refund window. It does mean that if you are buying because of a study you saw cited, the study and the gummy are not the same thing.

Not declared: how the 525 mg splits

The blend is disclosed as one total. US labelling rules allow that, but it means the amount of apple cider vinegar — the ingredient with the most human research behind it — is not published anywhere we could find, and neither is the split between the three BHB salts.

It contains corn syrup and cane sugar

The other ingredients are corn syrup, purified water, pure cane sugar, apple pectin, citric acid, sodium citrate, tapioca starch, natural flavors and beet root powder. That works out to 2 g of sugar per gummy, 1 g of it added. Small, but not zero — worth knowing if you are eating to a strict low-carb or keto target.

Distributed for BURNTIDE, 11870 62nd St N, Largo, FL 33773, with support@healthyconsumer.us given as the support address. The label carries an “Assembled in the USA” mark — that describes assembly, not approval or inspection of the finished gummy.

No stimulants on the ingredient list

The full label lists no caffeine, no green tea extract and no guarana. If you are sensitive to stimulants, or you already get them from coffee or a pre-workout earlier in the day, that is worth knowing — and it is the clearest claim on this product we can verify word for word against the jar.

Nothing on the label is a stimulant, so whatever this product does or does not do for you, it is not doing it with caffeine.
